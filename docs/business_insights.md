## Purpose

This document summarizes the business insights generated from the credit card fraud analytics project.

The goal is to convert analysis outputs into clear observations that can support fraud monitoring, reporting, and operational decision-making.

## Key Insights

| Insight Area | Observation | Business Meaning |
|---|---|---|
| Fraud Distribution | Fraudulent transactions represent a small share of total transactions | Fraud is less frequent but still important due to financial and customer impact |
| Transaction Amount | Fraud appears across different transaction values | Amount alone should not be used as the only review factor |
| Transaction Hour | Fraud activity varies across transaction hours | Time-based monitoring can help operations teams prioritize review windows |
| Transaction Location | Some locations show higher fraud activity | Location-level review can support targeted monitoring |
| Device Trust Score | Lower device trust scores appear more associated with fraud | Device-level signals can help identify risky transaction behavior |

## Business Interpretation

The analysis shows that fraud monitoring should not rely on one factor alone. A more useful review process combines multiple transaction characteristics such as location, transaction hour, transaction amount, and device trust score.

This approach gives business teams better visibility into fraud behavior and helps them decide where additional review may be needed.

## Recommendations

- Monitor fraud rate regularly as part of business reporting.
- Review transaction locations with higher fraud activity.
- Track fraud trends by transaction hour to identify unusual time-based patterns.
- Include device trust score in fraud review dashboards.
- Compare fraud and non-fraud transaction amount patterns before making operational decisions.
- Use simple reporting metrics before considering advanced analytical approaches.
